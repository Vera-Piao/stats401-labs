Semantic Analysis

Embeddings, Projection, and Topics

Each cleaned passage is encoded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 into a normalized 384-dimensional semantic vector. UMAP projects those vectors into two dimensions (n_neighbors = 15, min_dist = 0.15, cosine metric, random seed 401). K-means groups the original 384-dimensional embeddings into eight clusters (k = 8, random seed 401); labels are assigned after inspecting representative passages and the ten highest mean TF-IDF terms in each cluster.

Semantic Findings

What the Structure Reveals

  1. What are the major semantic topics?

    Eight interpretable topics emerge: STEM courses and prerequisites; academic credit and procedures; degree requirements and electives; politics, history, and global studies; media, arts, and culture; Chinese language and China studies; health, society, and research; and university policies and student life.

  2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

    Academic Credit and Procedures is the most visibly cross-cutting topic, appearing in nine Parts and linking curriculum, financial information, admissions, academic procedures, and the calendar. University Policies and Student Life spans eleven Parts, showing that institutional guidance is distributed throughout the bulletin rather than isolated in one chapter.

  3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

    Part 10, Majors and Courses, contains all eight topics and the broadest topic mixture. Among the smaller narrative sections, Part 2 and Part 3 each combine several themes, whereas Financial Information and the Calendar concentrate mainly on academic procedures.

  4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

    For example, passage p00002 in General Information and p00014 in A Liberal Arts Education are nearest neighbors because both describe DKU's research-oriented liberal arts mission. The map also connects p00003 on campus diversity with p00229 on cross-cultural student clubs, despite their different formal locations.

  5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?

    The compact grade-point table p00147 on page 52 is the only Academic Procedures passage assigned to the STEM Courses and Prerequisites cluster. Passage p00026 on advanced writing and speaking is also a visual outlier within The Curriculum because its language is closer to media and communication passages than to policy prose.

  6. Does the concept (credit, graduation, registration, or academic integrity) occur in one semantic region or across several topics?

    “Credit” occurs in 263 passages and is spread across all eight topics, led by degree requirements and academic procedures. “Graduation” appears 30 times, mostly in Academic Procedures; “registration” appears eight times, six of them in that same formal Part; and the exact phrase “academic integrity” appears twice, both in Academic Procedures. Design Description

Analytical and Interaction Choices

The analysis treats each paragraph-like bulletin passage as the semantic unit because it is long enough to express a policy or course idea but short enough to support passage-level retrieval. The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model turns each passage into a 384-dimensional embedding, and cosine similarity supplies the five nearest neighbors. UMAP is used only to create the two-dimensional display; clusters and neighbors are calculated in the original embedding space so that screen geometry does not redefine semantic similarity. Eight semantic topics were generated by applying K-means clustering (k = 8, random seed = 401) to the original 384-dimensional passage embeddings rather than the two-dimensional UMAP coordinates. Each cluster was then labeled by examining its representative passages and ten highest-scoring TF-IDF terms. The final labels summarize the dominant subject matter shared by the passages in each cluster.

The map encodes topic with a categorical color palette, passage length with area, and semantic proximity with position. Low default opacity keeps dense regions legible, while selection outlines the chosen passage and its neighbors. Search plus topic and formal-section filters support targeted questions and open exploration. Clicking reveals preserved metadata and original text, while zoom and pan make dense course clusters inspectable. The matrix offers a complementary aggregate view: rows retain the bulletin's formal Parts, columns show the eight data-driven topics, and color intensity encodes passage count. Coordination works in both directions, where a matrix cell filters points, and a selected point outlines its matching cell, which allows readers to move between document structure and individual evidence.
