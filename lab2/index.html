Lab 2 Assignment

City Population and Development

This lollipop chart compares city population, temperature, development level, and region.

Design Justification

I used a horizontal lollipop chart because it makes the populations of twelve cities easy to compare along a common numerical scale. Each city is placed on the vertical axis, while population is mapped to horizontal position and line length, so cities with larger populations appear farther to the right. Circle size represents average temperature, allowing warmer and cooler cities to be compared without adding another axis. Color represents region because region is a categorical variable, and distinct colors make the four groups easy to identify. Development level is included beside each city name and ordered from Low to High, which preserves the ordinal meaning of the variable. The light connecting lines guide the eye from each label to its population value. The tooltip provides exact values on demand while keeping the chart uncluttered. The chart suggests that highly developed cities generally have larger populations, while the temperature and region encodings help reveal differences among cities with similar population sizes.
